Habitat & Distribution

The Philippine Eagle is endemic to the Philippines, where it can be found on four major islands: eastern Luzon, Samar, Leyte, and Mindanao. The largest numbers of eagles reside on Mindanao. It thrives in dense, old-growth dipterocarp forests, typically in steep, mountainous areas. The loss of this habitat is the primary threat to its survival.

Diet

expand_more

The Philippine Eagle's diet varies from island to island. Its primary prey includes flying lemurs (colugos), but it also hunts small mammals like palm civets and monkeys, as well as snakes, monitor lizards, and even other birds of prey. They are apex predators in their forest ecosystem.

Conservation Efforts

expand_more

Numerous organizations, led by the Philippine Eagle Foundation, are working to save the species. Efforts include community-based conservation programs, habitat protection and reforestation, public awareness campaigns, and a comprehensive conservation breeding program. Reintroducing captive-bred eagles into protected areas is a key strategy for population recovery.
